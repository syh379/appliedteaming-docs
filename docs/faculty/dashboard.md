# The cohort dashboard

**What it is**: your view of the class as it moves through the semester. Everything here is **aggregated and k-anonymous**.

## What you see

![The faculty cohort dashboard.](/screenshots/faculty-dashboard.png)
*The class at a glance, participation, role and attribute trends, and teams needing a nudge.*

- **Participation**: teams, students, how many have checked in, and how many have finished every check-in.
- **Teams**: each team's check-in progress (how many check-ins the whole team has submitted) at a glance.
- **The class right now**: k-anonymous role and attribute distributions across every team.
- **Worth a conversation**: descriptive, name-free nudges (e.g., "2 of 4 submitted the mid-point check-in"), never a red flag on a named student.

Two buttons sit in the header: **Completion** (below) and **Roster & join code** (see [setup](/faculty/setup)).

## Completion, for grading

There is one thing faculty legitimately need by name: **who submitted each check-in and who didn't**, so you can grade on a completion basis. The **Completion** view is exactly that, a simple grid of every student against every check-in.

![The completion grid.](/screenshots/faculty-completion.png)
*Each student against each check-in: a ✓ for submitted, an amber — for not yet, a per-student count, and a class total per check-in. Grouped by team.*

This is the **one place a name appears** in the faculty view, and it deliberately shows **submission status only**, never the content of the reflection. Completion is an administrative fact you grade on; the goals, roles, attributes, and peer feedback themselves stay private and aggregate, exactly as everywhere else.

## A team, up close

You can open a single team for its aggregate picture and its **synthesis**, a plain-language reading of how the team works, drawn from the whole team's own checkpoint answers about the core attributes, the roles they played, and how they ranked those attributes.

![A per-team view.](/screenshots/faculty-team.png)
*A team's synthesis and its ranked attributes, aggregated from the team's own words, never an individual's answer by name.*

## Notes

- **No names on reflections. No attributed peer feedback. Ever.** Groups smaller than three are suppressed entirely.
- The dashboard is descriptive, not evaluative: it helps you notice where to check in, not rank students.
- It updates as students submit, so you can see momentum across the semester.
